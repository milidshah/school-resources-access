Proposal

Goals

This project intends to analyze issues of social justice in schools by exploring how specific resources in a school can influence the frequency of conflict, measured through the amount of disciplinary actions. This pressing issue highlights topics from our focus courses, such as the impact of social justice in education and the need for proper school resources to better support students of different backgrounds. We will analyze this issue through a data dashboard, followed by proposals to mitigate the impacts of the trends seen through the visualization. 

Dataset

The dataset we are using is the school_safety dataset from the United States Department of Education National Center for Educational Statistics (NCES). It can be found here, with the full PDF being located here. It contains 2762 observations or rows and 424 columns; interpreted in the context of the dataset, this means there is data for 424 different variables for 2762 schools. The data comes from a survey conducted 7 separate times by the Institute of Education Sciences since 1999. We chose the most recent dataset (covering the 2017-2018 school year) published for public use in 2020. The main function of this data is to represent characteristics that relate to safety resources of various schools across the United States. Each row represents a unique school and contains a multitude of variables relating to school safety and other school characteristics. For the purpose of simplifying this data, we will be focusing on 30 main variables to discuss school resources (the numbers in bracket correlate to the number identifying each variable in the dataset): 

  1. Social emotional learning training for students (35)

  2. Individual mentoring/tutoring/coaching by adults (37)

  3. Student involvement in restorative circles (40)

  4. Promote sense of community/social integration (41)

  5. Acceptance groups (44-46)

  6. Formal process to obtain parental input (47)

  7. Provide training or assistance to parents (48)

  8. Community Involvement (51 - 58) 

  9. Treatment at school by school-employed or contracted mental health professional (88)

  10. Teacher training (97-109)

These each can be combined to assess school resources (one factor we are observing in our research question). We will also use variables 182-185 to assess “school disciplinary actions” to represent the frequency of disciplinary actions in a school. 

Question

How does the availability of specific resources in a school, such as mental health support, acceptance groups, community involvement, etc. influence the frequency of disciplinary actions?

Motivation

In our classes focused on social justice in schools and Critical Race Theory in education, we have facilitated numerous discussions analyzing how various environmental factors can influence a student’s educational experience and learning outcomes. One particular topic we touched on was school disciplinary policies. Disciplinary actions, when administered unfairly or ineffectively, are known to be linked to the school-to-prison pipeline, a systemic problem funneling students, often from marginalized backgrounds, into the criminal justice system. By analyzing the influence of factors such as mental health support, acceptance groups, and community involvement on execution of school discipline, we can pinpoint potential resources to break this damaging cycle. Ultimately, addressing our research question has the potential to improve the educational experiences and life prospects of students in high-crime areas, leading to more equitable and successful educational outcomes.

Data Visualization & Impact

In order to answer our research question, we plan to learn how to create a data dashboard, which will tie in a new skill as well combine old skills we learned about making data visualizations (scatterplot, histogram, etc). We will first generate a composite school support resource variable by combining data variables capturing mental health support, acceptance groups, community involvement, etc. into a single Likert scale variable indicating the overall level of support resources schools possess. The main visualization will be a scatter plot that has the composite school support resource variable on the x axis and the frequency of school discipline on the y axis.  With this visualization, we intend to visualize preliminary trends in the influence of support resources on disciplinary actions. Next, we plan to make the data dashboard interactive, meaning that clicking on each bin will yield a unique visualization for specific support resource variable. These “sub-graphs” will show how each individual support resource compares to the frequency of disciplinary actions. 

In addition to this main visualization, we also plan on extending our analysis to school types. We’ll use the dashboard to separately visualize our findings in relation to location of schools (rural/urban) and category of schools (e.g. public, charter, magenet) – both variables that can be obtained from the same school_safety dataset. These visualizations could further help us identify trends in the distribution of support resources and characteristics of disciplinary actions across different school types, potentially informing policy developments. 

Overall, by using this data dashboard, we will be able to see whether the amount of resources a school has to support its students, impacts the frequencies of school discipline. This visualization not only might help us understand a potential problem in the correlation of discipline in a school to the amount of resources for student success, but it also might indicate ways to mitigate this issue, by honing in on certain variables (school resources) that lead to less need for school discipline. Overall, through this data dashboard, we will be able to better understand our current research question as well as take strides in applying our findings to learn more about common and pressing issues.
